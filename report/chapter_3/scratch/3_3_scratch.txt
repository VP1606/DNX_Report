The high \gls{snr} achieved through spatiotemporal denoising and max-normalisation 
confers several critical advantages for the stability and efficiency of the 
\texttt{SNN\_MAX\_REPLICA\_SE} architecture:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Prevention of LIF neuron saturation.} 
    In the absence of input conditioning, high-magnitude or noisy voxel values 
    can drive every neuron in the initial stem layer to fire continuously, 
    flooding the network with uninformative spikes. By normalising the voxel 
    tensor to the range $[0, 1]$, the input magnitude is constrained such that 
    only neurons encoding genuine structural features --- such as vehicle edges 
    and motion boundaries --- exceed their firing threshold, preserving the 
    sparsity and selectivity of the deeper backbone stages \cite{eshraghian2021training}.

    \item \textbf{Improved objectness discrimination.}
    Background artifacts inherent to \gls{hd} event cameras, such as flickering 
    illumination and sensor noise, generate spurious event clusters that are 
    geometrically similar to small objects. By rejecting these low-activity 
    events during the \gls{baf} denoising step, the network is not required to 
    expend representational capacity on learning to suppress noise. Instead, 
    the full parameter budget of the backbone can be directed towards learning 
    the geometric structure of real vehicles \cite{eshraghian2021training, roy2019towards}.

    \item \textbf{Membrane potential stability and temporal coding fidelity.}
    \gls{lif} neurons integrate input current over time to build their membrane 
    potential towards a firing threshold. When the input signal is corrupted 
    by noise, the membrane potential undergoes stochastic fluctuations, 
    degrading the temporal precision of the resulting spike train. A high 
    \gls{snr} input ensures that the membrane potential accumulates in a stable, 
    monotonic trajectory, allowing neurons to fire at the precise moment 
    a spatial feature passes through their receptive field and producing 
    temporally consistent bounding box predictions across the 100ms window 
    \cite{eshraghian2021training}.

    \item \textbf{Surrogate gradient stability during training.}
    Directly trained \glspl{snn} rely on surrogate gradients to circumvent 
    the non-differentiability of the spiking activation function during 
    backpropagation \cite{neftci2019surrogate}. Noisy forward-pass activations 
    corrupt the loss landscape, producing high-variance gradient estimates 
    that destabilise optimisation. A clean, high-\gls{snr} input signal 
    produces a smoother loss surface, enabling the Adam optimiser to converge 
    reliably and contributing to the steady improvement in detection 
    performance observed during training \cite{neftci2019surrogate, eshraghian2021training}.

    \item \textbf{Reduced synaptic operations and neuromorphic energy efficiency.}
    Since \glspl{snn} consume energy only upon spike emission, every spurious 
    background event that is suppressed during preprocessing directly reduces 
    the number of \glspl{sop} at inference time. This establishes a direct 
    link between preprocessing quality and the power efficiency of the 
    architecture when deployed on neuromorphic hardware \cite{roy2019towards}.

\end{itemize}
