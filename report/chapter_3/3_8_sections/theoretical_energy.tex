\subsubsection*{Critical Analysis}

Despite the relatively high per-operation routing overhead inherent to early-generation neuromorphic chips, deploying the \gls{snn} on an Intel Loihi processor yields a $65.12\%$ in energy consumption per frame compared to running the continuous baseline on the same NVIDIA A100 GPU used for training. This outcome validates the hypothesis formulated in Section \ref{subsubsec:energy_hypothesis}.

The fundamental driver of this efficiency is the massive reduction in computational workload: the \gls{snn} requires only $2.67$ G-\glspl{sop} per frame, compared to the $9.22$ G-\glspl{flop} required by the \gls{cnn} baseline --- $71.02\%$ reduction in total operations. 
However, a critical observation emerges when comparing the workload reduction ($71.02\%$) to the projected energy savings ($65.12\%$).
This discrepancy represents the "routing overhead" penalty of first-generation neuromorphic silicon: inter-core spike communication is an extremely complex task on chips like Loihi, and as such causes
a single \gls{sop} to cost significantly more energy ($20.4\%$ more) than a \gls{flop} on a highly optimised \gls{gpu} such as the NVIDIA A100 \cite{su2023coreinterfaceoptimizationmulticore}.
The \gls{snn} therefore achieves its dominant efficiency purely through extreme activation sparsity, rather than hardware-level architectural optimisations.

Furthermore, this 65\% energy savings was achieved despite the integration of the high-resolution G3 backbone ($22 \times 40$ feature maps). This proves that the biological sparsity of the \gls{snn} is robust enough to sustain high-definition spatial reasoning without inducing a catastrophic power surge---a scaling feat that is mathematically impossible for dense \gls{cnn} architectures.

Finally, it must be noted that comparing experimental neuromorphic silicon against a mature, massively parallel and optimised NVIDIA \gls{gpu} gives us an upper-bound on the energy savings of event-driven \glspl{snn}, rather than a realistic value.
As neuromorphic hardware develops, the per-\gls{sop} energy cost will continue to converge towards the theoretical \acrfull{cmos} limit established by Horowitz \cite{horowitz20141}, and the power savings will scale exponentially higher towards the optimal $94.33\%$ value obtained in Section \ref{subsubsec:mean_energy_savings}.

Overall, the predicted power consumption on the various platforms described in Table \ref{tab:hardware_extrapolation} serves as conclusive proof that event-driven \glspl{snn} can bring highly power-efficient computing to resource-constrained edge environments, validating the neuromorphic paradigm as a viable, sustainable solution for real-time autonomous traffic monitoring.

\subsubsection*{Energy Efficiency Comparison}

The following experiment evaluates the energy efficiency frontier, where efficiency can be defined as $\text{efficiency} = \frac{mAP_{50}}{\text{Power Per Frame}}$,
of \texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN} against contemporary published \gls{snn} detectors.

However, it is critical to explicitly acknowledge the evaluation disparities between these projects.
The competitor models are evaluated over the 80-class COCO dataset, consisting of dense, static RGB images.
Achieving a high \gls{map_50} on COCO requires
significantly more model capacity than the single-class (Vehicle) detection task evaluated
here on \gls{etram}. Consequently, the raw \gls{map_50} scores are not directly comparable as
equivalent measures of architectural capability.

As demonstrated in Table \ref{tab:efficiency_landscape},
\texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN} establishes a profound new benchmark for neuromorphic efficiency, achieving an \gls{map_50}/mJ ratio of
$24.92$ -- nearly an order of magnitude (approximately $8\times$) higher than the most efficient competitor, \texttt{SpikeDet-S} ($3.19$).

To attempt to draw a conclusion from these results despite the evaluation differences, a worst-case sensitivity check is instructive.
Even applying an aggressive $50\%$ penalty to \texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN}'s \gls{map_50} ($59.8 \rightarrow 29.9$) to simulate a hypothetical degradation under an
$80$-class regime, the resulting $12.46$ \gls{map_50}/mJ ratio still exceeds the
next-best published entry (SpikeDet-S at $3.19$) by a factor of $3.9\times$.
Furthermore, this lead cannot be attributed to model scale: 
\texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN} is the smallest model in the table at $11.7$M
parameters, and even the closest comparator by parameter count,
SpikeYOLO-N at $13.2$M, registers an order-of-magnitude higher
synaptic-operation budget per frame ($25.1$ vs $2.7$ G-\glspl{sop}).

This efficiency advantage is the cumulative product of two architectural decisions made to develop the \texttt{G3} iteration of \texttt{DetectorNX}.
First, the integration of \texttt{SEWResBlock} units within the backbone preserves activation sparsity deep into the network by guaranteeing binary information flow without inducing a spike explosion.
In combination with the \gls{frr} to push the firing rate to under $20\%$, both of these mechanisms reduce the total \gls{sop} count.
In addition to this, the lightweight multi-box detection head avoids heavier architectures such as the transformer-based or anchor-rich used by other competitors, 
removing a major source of dense computation from the model.

In conclusion, the comparative analysis presented in Table \ref{tab:efficiency_landscape} demonstrates that
\texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN} achieves the highest \gls{map_50}/mJ ratio among the surveyed \gls{snn} object detectors,
delivering $24.92$ \gls{map_50}/mJ --- a $7.8\times$ lead over the next-best published entry.
Even under the abstracted multi-class adjustment described above, this lead persists by a factor of $3.9\times$.
Whilst the dataset asymmetry between \gls{etram} and COCO precludes a strict head-to-head claim, the sheer magnitude of this lead validates the project's central design philosophy: that paradigm-level sparsity, when combined with deliberate architectural restraint, can deliver order-of-magnitude efficiency gains in the resource-constrained edge-deployment scenarios that motivated this work.
