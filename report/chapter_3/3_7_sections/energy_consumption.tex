\subsection{Theoretical Energy Consumption}
\label{subsec:energy_consumption}

The Theoretical Energy Consumption is very similar in nature to the Dynamic Power experiment discussed in Section \ref{subsec:dynamic_power}.
It uses the same principles and formulae for \glspl{sop} and \glspl{flop},
but differs in the final calculation:
as opposed to calculating the dynamic power consumption per frame, this experiment aims to find the average power consumption per frame across the entire dataset, and extrapolates for multiple hardware platforms:

\begin{equation}
\bar{E}_{SNN} = \sum_{l=1}^{L} (\mathrm{SOPs}_l \times E_{chip})
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \bar{E}_{CNN} = \sum_{l=1}^{L} (\mathrm{FLOPs}_l \times E_{MAC})
\end{equation}

\noindent The calculation mirrors Equation \ref{eq:e_snn}, however $E_{AC}$ is replaced by $E_{chip}$, the per-\gls{sop} energy of the target hardware platform, as listed in Table \ref{tab:energy_constants}.

Subsequently, the total percentage energy saving is formulated using the following equation:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Energy Savings} = \left(1 - \frac{\bar{E}_{SNN}}{\bar{E}_{CNN}} \right) \times 100\%
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Hypothesis}
\label{subsubsec:energy_hypothesis}

We hypothesise that the \gls{snn} will demonstrate a metabolic advantage of at 
least one order of magnitude ($>90\%$) over the \gls{cnn} baseline, predicated 
on two complementary properties of the $\bar{E}_{SNN}$ formulation:

\begin{enumerate}

    \item \textbf{Activation sparsity:} By exploiting the sparsity of the 
    \gls{etram} event stream, the backbone is expected to maintain $\zeta < 5\%$, 
    bypassing the majority of computations that the \gls{cnn} performs densely 
    regardless of scene activity.

    % \item \textbf{Hardware-level efficiency:} Replacing continuous \gls{mac} operations 
    % with binary \gls{ac} operations yields a fundamental reduction in per-operation energy 
    % cost at the silicon level \cite{horowitz20141}.

    \item \textbf{Mathematical simplification:} Replacing \gls{mac} operations 
    with binary \gls{ac} operations yields a $5\times$ reduction in per-operation 
    energy cost ($0.9\text{ pJ}$ vs $4.6\text{ pJ}$), which is expected to 
    provide a substantial efficiency dividend even when accounting for the $T=10$ 
    temporal overhead \cite{horowitz20141}.

\end{enumerate}

When extrapolated to physical neuromorphic platforms, the \gls{snn}'s event-driven 
resting state is expected to yield an exponential power advantage over synchronous \gls{gpu} 
accelerators. With Intel Loihi 2, the neuromorphic paradigm benefits from 
both extreme activation sparsity and a lower per-operation cost ($16.1\text{ pJ}$ 
per \gls{sop}) compared to the per-\gls{flop} cost of the benchmark NVIDIA A100 ($19.6\text{ pJ}$) 
documented in Table \ref{tab:energy_constants} \cite{gralewicz2024spikes, merolla2014million, a100_usage}.
