\section{Architectural Evolution}
\label{sec:architectural_evolution}

The final \texttt{G3} revision of the \texttt{DetectorNX} architecture was not the result of a single design phase,
but rather the culmination of an iterative engineering process across four distinct generations.
According to the project's success criteria, the model required both high temporal precision and robust spatial localization.
This section documents the empirical obstacles encountered during development, and the actionable insights that led to the final optimised configuration.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% GENERATION 0 %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Generation 0: The Spatial Cropping Pitfall (Early Prototyping)}

\subsubsection*{1. Architectural Intent}

Prior to the implementation of global downsampling, early prototyping explored the use of aggressive spatial cropping to bypass the computational costs of high-definition event processing.

The hypothesis was based on improving the \gls{snr} at the network's input:
by cropping the raw $1280 \times 720$ \gls{etram} sensor data into deterministic $224 \times 224$ patches anchored to the target vehicle,
the initial stem layers would receive a significantly higher frequency of spikes per pixel.
%
This \textit{localised foveation} aimed to preserve fine geometric detail without incurring the memory overhead of processing the full \gls{hd} frame.

\subsubsection*{2. Empirical Result}
During training on the cropped datasets, the model appeared highly successful, 
easily converging to an apparent validation \gls{miou} of $> 70\%$.
%
However, when this trained model was evaluated against raw, uncropped full frames,
performance collapsed to an effective \gls{miou} of near zero.

\subsubsection*{3. Diagnostic Analysis: Constant Target Bias}
Analysing the output frames revealed that the network had suffered from \textit{Constant Target Bias}.
Because the pipeline used deterministic, centroid-anchored cropping, the target vehicle occupied the exact geometric centre of every training sample.
As a result, the regression head ignored the sparse event stream and learned to output a constant coordinate vector instead.
%
This bias was invisible during validation because the validation split inherited the same cropping method;
it only appeared during full-frame inference, where the predicted box persistently anchored to
the image centre regardless of the vehicle's true dynamic position, as
visually shown in Figure~\ref{fig:constant_target_bias}.

\subsubsection*{4. Actionable Insight and Iteration}
The \textit{Constant Target Bias} failure proved that deterministic cropping destroys the spatial variance required to train a regression head.
To force the network to actively locate targets, the localised foveation had to be abandoned entirely.
Consequently, Generation 1 (G1) was designed to process the
entire global frame, relying on aggressive spatial downsampling rather
than cropping.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% GENERATION 1 %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Generation 1 (G1): The Multi-Box Paradigm Shift}

\subsubsection*{1. Architectural Intent}
After removing spatial cropping, the architecture progressed to full-frame global downsampling.
The G1 prototype attempted to process this global frame using the standard single-box
regression head (outputting a single $[x, y, w, h]$ vector per frame).

The hypothesis underpinning the choice of a single-box regression head was driven by computational cost:
for simple automotive perception using a focused, forward-facing camera,
a single bounding box could theoretically encompass vehicles ahead to draw a simplified ``critical safety region''.
If successful, this would bypass the heavy computational overhead and complexity required by a multi-box detection head.
%
In hindsight, this assumption was structurally flawed for the specific domain of autonomous driving and \gls{adas},
which requires precise tracking of every vehicle independently for safe path planning.

\subsubsection*{2. Empirical Result}
The single-box model proved incapable of handling the multi-vehicle traffic scenes from the \gls{etram} dataset,
and unable to accurately draw the ``critical region'' so that all vehicles present were covered by one box.
The model failed to converge on distinct targets, instead outputting a meaningless ``super-box'' that
hovered in the empty space between vehicles or encompassed the entire roadway.

\subsubsection*{3. Diagnostic Analysis: Super-Box}
This failure was determined to be a case of \textit{Single-Target Ambiguity and Label Noise}.
The deep backbone naturally extracted features for all visible vehicles in the global frame.
However, because the regression head could only output one bounding box,
the single-target label penalised the network if it correctly detected a secondary vehicle.
To minimise the loss, the network averaged the spatial coordinates of all active spike clusters, producing the floating ``super-box'' artefact:
a single bounding box that encompasses all vehicles and roadways rather than individual targets in isolation, as visually evidenced in
Figure~\ref{fig:superbox_failure}.

\subsubsection*{4. Actionable Insight and Iteration}
Generation 1 confirmed that a single global regression head is incompatible with uncropped automotive perception, which requires precise and isolated detection of all obstacles present in the scene.
To resolve this, the network's output topology shifted to a multi-box regression head;
the output was expanded to a fixed-capacity $[B, 5, 5]$ tensor, predicting 5 boxes per inference,
each augmented with a fifth ``Objectness'' confidence score $(x, y, w, h, c)$.
%
To support this, Generation 2 (G2) introduces a Vectorised Matching Loss to match predictions to their respective ground-truth annotations.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% GENERATION 2 %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Generation 2 (G2): The Expanded Spatial Head (7.00M Parameters)}

\subsubsection*{1. Architectural Intent}
As mandated by the results and analysis of Generation 1, Generation 2 (G2) introduced the multi-box regression head.
This replaced the global output vector with a fixed-capacity $[B, 5, 5]$ tensor, predicting 5 boxes per inference alongside the aforementioned ``Objectness'' score $(x, y, w, h, c)$.

To train this new multi-box paradigm, G2 implemented the \textit{Vectorised Matching Loss}.
This algorithm computed a 3D matrix of \gls{iou} overlaps between the 5 predictions and $M$ ground-truth
targets, using greedy matching to assign coordinate gradients.
%
Unmatched predicted boxes were assigned a target confidence of $0.0$ to define a penalty for false positives.
To feed this new regression head, the deep backbone features were compressed using an Adaptive Average Pooling layer of $4 \times 4$.

\subsubsection*{2. Empirical Result}
G2 successfully resolved the ``super-box'' error and learned to predict distinct, separate bounding boxes,
however validation revealed a new failure: high false-positive rates and overlapping predictions.
The network's Average Detections per Frame (ADP) rose to $2.50$, significantly higher than the true \gls{etram} dataset average of $2.02$.

\subsubsection*{3. Diagnostic Analysis: Information Congestion}
The hallucination of extra vehicles was determined to be \textit{Information Congestion} within the regression head.
Using the $4 \times 4$ Adaptive Pooling layer compressed the field of view into just 16 spatial blocks, creating a severe spatial bottleneck.
%
When vehicles were in close proximity, or near clusters of sensor noise, their respective spiking features were
averaged into the same $4 \times 4$ block;
%
the regression head lacked the spatial memory required to distinguish legitimate target spike-trains from adjacent background noise,
leading it to output multiple overlapping boxes for a single stimulus.

\subsubsection*{4. Actionable Insight and Iteration}
G2 proved that a multi-box head requires a finer structural map of backbone activations to perform Object-Noise-Discrimination, which
isolates vehicle spike trains from surrounding sensor noise.
%
To resolve this, the pooling resolution was quadrupled from $4 \times 4$ to $8 \times 8$.

Initial prototyping of the wider pool size revealed a new limitation in the model's architecture,
with the Mean \gls{iou} plateauing at $41\%$.
%
This confirmed that the backbone itself was downsampling the raw sensor data too aggressively,
leading to the development of Generation 3 (G3), which expands both the head's pooling capacity and the backbone's semantic depth.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%% GENERATION 3 %%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Generation 3 (G3): The High-Resolution Backbone (11.7M Parameters)}

\subsubsection*{1. Architectural Intent}
The objective of this final generation was to break the $41\%$ \gls{miou} precision ceiling established at the end of Generation 2.
Analysis of the network's internal spiking dynamics revealed two distinct flaws that constrained its geometric precision:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Compression:}
    The G2 backbone downsampled the feature map to a resolution of $11 \times 20$.
    At this scale, a single pixel in the final feature map represented a massive $16 \times 16$ area of the original HD sensor.
    As a result, the network was blind to fine-grained pixel shifts, since it lacked the geometric detail required to draw tight, accurate bounding boxes.

    \item \textbf{Neural Silencing:}
    The G2 backbone collapsed to an average firing rate of $< 1.5\%$.
    This extreme sparsity starved the regression head of the temporal evidence needed for high-confidence predictions, preventing the network from fully utilising its 10-timestep observation window.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{2. Actionable Implementation: Semantic Expansion and Awakening}
To solve these bottlenecks, the architecture was overhauled to create the final G3 configuration:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Resolution Boost and Semantic Depth:} To provide the necessary geometric detail, the convolution stride in Block 3 was reduced to $s=1$.
    This effectively halted the downsampling earlier in the network, doubling the final feature map resolution to $22 \times 40$ (quadrupling the available spatial cells to 880).
    To handle the increased complexity and high-frequency sensor artefacts exposed by this higher resolution, the backbone's semantic capacity was expanded via the addition of a 4th \texttt{SEWResBlock} (512 channels). 
    This allowed the model to reason more deeply about the structural composition of the vehicle.
    
    \item \textbf{Metabolic Regularisation:} 
    The \gls{lif} firing threshold was lowered ($V_{th}=0.5 \rightarrow 0.2$) to wake up the silent backbone, making it significantly easier for neurons to activate. 
    Concurrently, the \gls{frr} penalty multiplier ($\lambda$) was also increased ($1.0 \rightarrow 5.0$) with a target rate of 20\%. 
    This forced the network to generate a dense, high-frequency stream of geometric data, providing the regression head with the robust temporal evidence needed to sense the exact boundaries of the vehicles.
    
    \item \textbf{Hardware Optimisation:} 
    Expanding the parameter count to 11.7M and quadrupling the spatial resolution exponentially increased the computational load. 
    %
    To prevent latency penalties, the architecture was refactored to utilise SpikingJelly's \texttt{step\_mode='m'} (\textit{Fused-Kernel optimisation}) \cite{fang2023spikingjelly}.
    Rather than using a slow, sequential Python loop to evaluate the network across $T=10$ timesteps, this method passes the entire spatiotemporal tensor
    directly to custom \gls{cuda} kernels, allowing the temporal dynamics to be executed in parallel on the \gls{gpu}.
    %
    This was coupled with \gls{amp} utilising 16-bit tensor cores, resulting in a $\sim 1.8\times$ training speedup and making the G3 configuration computationally feasible to develop.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{3. Final Empirical Result}
The G3 overhaul successfully broke the precision ceiling. 
By preserving spatial resolution and enforcing metabolic activity, the network achieved a peak validation \gls{miou} of $46.1\%$ and \gls{map_50} of $59.8\%$ (detailed fully in Chapter~\ref{cha:results_evaluation}).
This architecture was consequently locked as the definitive \texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN} model used for all final hardware
extrapolations and baseline comparisons.
