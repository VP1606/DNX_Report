\section{Report Structure}
\label{sec:intro_report_structure}

The report is structured to first establish the necessary theoretical foundation, before detailing the architecture and its empirical evaluation:

\paragraph{Background:}

\noindent Chapter~\ref{cha:background} provides the prerequisite theory for the \texttt{DetectorNX} project. It establishes \gls{cnn} and \gls{snn} fundamentals, event-based vision, and neuromorphic energy economics, before concluding with related work to define the critical gap in high-definition automotive spiking detection that this project addresses.

\paragraph{Methodology and Evaluation:}

\noindent Chapter~\ref{cha:methodology} details the technical implementation and its results. By integrating the architectural design (\texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN} and \texttt{DetectorNX-G3-CNN}) with the experimental setup and evaluation within a single chapter, the report directly links architectural choices—such as the shared multi-box head and \gls{bolt} pipeline—to their empirical outcomes across accuracy, latency, and energy dimensions.

\paragraph{Conclusions:}

\noindent Chapter~\ref{cha:summary_conclusions} closes the report by evaluating the overall project against the success criteria defined in Section~\ref{sec:intro_success_criteria}. It synthesises the principal findings, reflects critically on experimental limitations, and outlines future work, such as physical neuromorphic deployment and alternative spike encodings.
