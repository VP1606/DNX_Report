\section{Overview}
\label{sec:intro_overview}

Autonomous perception is one of the most critical frontiers in automotive engineering,
driven by legislation and consumer demand for increasingly complex autonomous driving systems requiring real-time spatial understanding.
Euro NCAP (Europe's vehicle safety rating programme) has published 2026 protocols, for example, that require advanced Autonomous Emergency Braking (AEB) to handle increasingly complex scenarios such as blind junctions, and mark a paradigm shift from reactive to predictive intervention \cite{EuroNCAP2025FrontalCollisions}.
It also introduces an all-new requirement for these more complex \gls{adas} to operate at speeds up to $130$\,km/h, which will require lower inference latencies \cite{EuroNCAP_SafeDriving_VehicleAssistance_2025}.

Modern vehicles rely on dense \acrfullpl{ann} to process high-definition video streams in real-time,
executing billions of \acrfullpl{flop} every second.
As a result, scaling such systems to handle more complex scenarios has caused both energy and thermal demands to grow substantially.
\textcite{lin_adas_cnn_issues} documented CPU-based \gls{cnn} inference exceeding the thermal and power constraints of \gls{adas} hardware, motivating the industry's shift to \gls{gpu}-class accelerators; 
continued growth in perception complexity now places dense \gls{ann} workloads on \glspl{gpu} on a similar trajectory, motivating a paradigm shift towards fundamentally more efficient architectures.

This project investigates a cutting-edge neuromorphic paradigm, \acrfullpl{snn}, and their feasibility in this domain.
To achieve this, the project develops \texttt{DetectorNX-G3-SNN}, a high-definition spiking architecture designed for multi-object automotive perception. 
While recent architectures such as SpikeDet \cite{fan2025spikedet}, SpikeYOLO \cite{spike_yolo}, and EMS-YOLO \cite{ems_yolo} have advanced \gls{snn} detection on general-purpose benchmarks, their applicability and efficiency trade-offs in high-definition automotive environments remain critically underexplored.
By rigorously benchmarking this system against a strictly equivalent continuous \gls{cnn} on the event-based \acrfull{etram} dataset \parencite{Verma_2024_CVPR}, 
this research empirically deconstructs the performance-efficiency frontier---measuring the precise trade-offs between the energy savings of event-driven dynamics and the spatial precision constraints of 1-bit temporal quantisation.
